# MOTIVATIONS
When I think of collecting data, I think about how people use that data. When thinking of data, large numbers come to mind, at least to me. We often think of data as this extremely complex concept that scientists and engineers work with. On the other hand, data is present everywhere, and in everything. It can be both quantitative and qualitative, which reminded me of social media platforms where all of the content we produce and consume is data. Our names, our lists of followers and following, the images and stories we publish, the content we like and share are all data. In this case, the way that it is used it to keep on the platform way longer by building an algorithm that keeps track of that data. That is why I was motivated to create a flask application that collected people's personal information. 

The internet is now a sea of data. There is nothing that you cannot find and everything that has ever been imported to the web is still there. In fact, mySpace was founded in 2003 and fell off around 2009, due to Facebook's ascension that created harsh competition between both platforms. Users could add friends, share pictures and thoughts, similar to a blog, and more. One unique trait is that they could even go as far as to change the layout through HTML and CSS so that their page was one-of-a-kind. However, despite its old age, the impact of mySpace still remains. Who knows what social media today would've looked like had it not been for the foundation of mySpace? Indeed, the mere concept of that social media was just reimagined for websites like Instagram and Facebook. People from different generation still create accounts on Facebook, Instagram, Tiktok, Youtube, Twitter, Pinterest, and more to find a place to organize their identity, which is a data itself. 

# INTENTIONS
That is why, for the first mini-project, I wanted to create something similar to mySpace. The data that was going to be fetched and stored would be people's personal information. The visual experience would be different than how mySpace once looked, because I wanted to act as if this app was a new social media page that was indirectly inspired by mySpace's concept.

# EXPECTED OUTCOMES
The expected outcome was that people could put in their own information such as their name, birthdate, pronouns; favorite interests, color, music, movies and shows, games, books; and other random details like the reason for their usage of the platform, their relationship status, their sexuality, ethnicity, zodiac sign and occupation.
